Gamaka Explorer

கமகம் — the soul of the swara

A plain note is only the beginning. In Carnatic music, swaras come alive through gamakas — the oscillations, slides and stresses that make the violin sing like a voice. Watch the pitch travel and hear each ornament for yourself.

What is a gamaka?கமகம்

A gamaka is the graceful movement into, around or out of a swara. Instead of landing flatly on a note and stopping, the player bends, oscillates or slides it — and that movement is what gives a raga its colour and feeling.

On the violin this is done entirely with the left hand: the finger rocks on the string, glides up or down, or pulls the pitch slightly higher. The bow keeps a steady, singing tone while the finger paints the ornament. The very same swara can sound completely different depending on the gamaka it carries — which is why two ragas can share the same notes yet feel worlds apart.

The ten classical gamakasதச வித கமகம்

Old texts describe dasavidha gamakam — ten kinds of gamaka. Many are specialised, and the practical ornaments above are the ones a student meets first. The classical ten are usually listed as:

  • 1Aarohanam — ascending movement
  • 2Avarohanam — descending movement
  • 3Dhaalu — a leap or jump to a swara
  • 4Sphuritam — a quick lower grace-touch
  • 5Kampitam — oscillation around a swara
  • 6Aahatam — striking up to a swara
  • 7Pratyaahatam — striking down to a swara
  • 8Tripuchcham — a three-fold ornament
  • 9Aandolam — a slow, wide swing
  • 10Murchana — a graded run of swaras
A note for the Guru: gamaka names and their exact shapes vary between traditions and textbooks, and the sounds here are gentle illustrations made with a synthesised violin voice — not a substitute for listening to and imitating a teacher. Please review the names, Tamil terms and demonstrations against your own tradition before sharing this with students.
